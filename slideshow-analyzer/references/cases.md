## Case 001: Hamster "Life lately" (soft-ad for MuseOn)

**Archetype**: Character-Action | **Trigger**: Emotional (meme/humor) | **Info Format**: List | **Visual Continuity**: Weak (thematic)

**Format**: 5 slides, 1080x1440 (3:4), AI-generated photorealistic hamster

| # | Role | Content | Text |
|---|------|---------|------|
| 0 | Hook | 4-panel grid preview | "Life lately:" |
| 1 | Spike | Hamster in cubicle giving middle finger to monitor | "Work" |
| 2 | Narrative | Hamster lifting dumbbells, wearing cap | "Fitness" |
| 3 | Narrative | Hamster on subway escalator with backpack | "Commute" |
| 4 | Soft-ad | Hamster in bed looking at iPad (MuseOn) | "Using MuseOn to reverse-engineer viral formulas" |

**Why it works**: The hamster is a safe proxy for workplace rebellion. The absurd scale makes it clearly a joke, giving the viewer permission to express real frustration.

```
Primary mechanism: "I wish I could do that" (Forbidden Release)
- Viewer emotion: Catharsis — "I wish I could flip off my computer at work"
- Sharing motive: "Look at this hamster doing what we all want to do"
- Unspoken truth: "I hate my job but I have to pretend I don't"

Secondary mechanism: "That doesn't belong there" (Absurd Intrusion)
- Role: Tiny hamster in human environments creates visual comedy base
  that makes the forbidden gesture land harder (cute + rude = funnier than just rude)
```

**Contrast dimensions**: All slides hit 3-4 dimensions (Scale + Behavior/Species-role + Context).

**Why it failed**: No mechanism was activated. A dog lying on a desk doesn't trigger any mechanism — nothing forbidden, no emotion projected, nothing absurd.

## Case 004: Cat "So how has your life been going" (soft-ad for VOMO)

**Archetype**: Character-Action | **Trigger**: Emotional (resonance + meme) | **Info Format**: Story (escalating) | **Visual Continuity**: Weak (thematic)

**Format**: 8 slides, ~1080x1080 (1:1), real cat photos (slides 0-6) + AI-generated (slide 7)

| # | Role | Content | Text |
|---|------|---------|------|
| 0 | Hook | White cat squinting judgmentally | "So how has your life been going" |
| 1 | Spike | Black kitten with huge eyes + loading spinner | "Brain not braining" |
| 2 | Narrative | Black cat in corner, physics equations around | "Study not studying" |
| 3 | Narrative | Tabby kitten with earbuds, eyes closed | "Sleep not sleeping" |
| 4 | Narrative | Tabby cat guarding one tiny coin with both paws | "Money not moneying" |
| 5 | Emotional climax | White cat extreme close-up, teary eyes | "Nobody loving" |
| 6 | Emotional climax | Kitten wrapped in blanket, big sad eyes | "Everybody hating" |
| 7 | Soft-ad | AI orange cat on sunset beach, VOMO logo | "thank god VOMO locked in, life is lifing again" |

**Why it works**: Cats are emotional proxies. The "X not X-ing" format gives viewers a safe, humorous language to describe real pain. Sharing is a way of saying "I'm struggling" while maintaining the social armor of humor.

```
Primary mechanism: "Say it for me" (Proxy Expression)
- Viewer emotion: "Finally someone said it" — relief from having messy life acknowledged
- Sharing motive: "This is literally me" — self-identification without vulnerability
- Unspoken truth: "Everything in my life is falling apart but I can't say that out loud"

Secondary mechanism: "It keeps getting worse" (Escalating Doom Spiral)
- Role: Descent structure (brain → study → sleep → money → love → hate) mirrors
  real overwhelm and creates dark humor catharsis
```

**Key insight**: Proxy Expression does NOT require animals. It requires: (1) a subject that can receive emotional projection, (2) a linguistic format that makes pain funny, (3) a descent/escalation structure.

## Case 005: Desk Objects "5 stages of writing" (FAILED)

**What was attempted**: Inanimate desk objects as characters in a doom spiral about writing.

**Why it failed**: Chose "Say it for me" (Proxy Expression) but used inanimate objects. Objects cannot carry Proxy Expression — no natural expressiveness, no visual humor per slide.

**Lesson**: (1) Vehicle-mechanism compatibility is non-negotiable. (2) The "cover the text" test is the most important quality gate. (3) Objects CAN be funny — but only in absurd/impossible states (keyboard growing mushrooms, coffee mug cracked and leaking).

## Case 006: Monkey Plush "Come Closer" (soft-ad for Kicker AI)

**Archetype**: Tension-Punchline | **Trigger**: Instinct (curiosity) + Emotional (meme) | **Info Format**: Zoom progression | **Visual Continuity**: Strong (zoom)

**Format**: 5 slides, portrait (~3:4), real phone photos of IKEA orangutan plush

| # | Role | Content | Text |
|---|------|---------|------|
| 0 | Hook | Full body plush on bed, pink bow, hands clasped | "Hey, come closer, i have to tell you something." |
| 1 | Build | Medium shot, sage green wall + wainscoting | "closer" |
| 2 | Build | Close-up face, teal wallpaper + bookshelf | "closer" |
| 3 | False arrival | Extreme close-up face, smirk fills frame | "there" |
| 4 | Punchline/Ad | Same extreme close-up, Kicker AI logo | "manual work is monkey behavior. smart people use Kicker AI." |

**Why it works**: The zoom progression makes swiping = getting closer. The viewer's physical gesture becomes part of the content. The ad IS the punchline — the monkey calling something "monkey behavior" is self-referential humor that makes the ad structurally inseparable from the joke.

```
Primary mechanism: "Come closer, I have a secret" (Parasocial Bait-and-Switch)
- Viewer emotion: Curiosity → amused surprise → "I got played but it was fun"
- Sharing motive: "I fell for this, now you fall for it too" (chain prank)
- Unspoken truth: "I don't mind being sold to if you make me laugh first"

Secondary mechanism: Self-Referential Humor
- Role: Monkey calling something "monkey behavior" makes the ad land
  without feeling like a sellout
```

**Key tactics**:
- **Zoom progression (strong continuity)**: Each slide is a tighter crop. Swiping = zooming in.
- **Conversational parasocial hook**: "Come closer" instructs the viewer to do what swiping already does.
- **False arrival + pattern break**: Slide 3 signals arrival; Slide 4 breaks zoom with text punchline.
- **Fixed expression reinterpretation**: Same stitched face reads differently at different zoom levels.

### Commercial Analysis

- **Ad location**: Slide 4, text overlay on same extreme close-up
- **Strategy**: Ad-as-punchline — "monkey behavior" pun makes the ad the joke itself
- **Narrative justification**: The monkey calling manual work "monkey behavior" is self-referential; removing the ad removes the humor
- **Visual space**: Text overlaid on the plush face (same frame as slide 3)
- **Subtlety score**: 2 (integrated — ad IS the punchline)
- **Product fit**: Works for any productivity/automation tool with a name or tagline that can create a pun with the vehicle

### Soul vs Skin

| Element | Soul/Skin | Value |
|---------|:---------:|-------|
| Trigger type | Soul | Instinct (curiosity) + Emotional (meme) |
| Primary mechanism | Soul | Parasocial Bait-and-Switch |
| Archetype | Soul | Tension-Punchline |
| Information Format | Soul | Zoom progression |
| Visual Continuity | Soul | Strong (zoom) — removing breaks the "getting closer" experience |
| Linguistic pattern | Soul | Imperative parasocial ("come closer") |
| Ad insertion strategy | Soul | Ad-as-punchline — the self-referential pun IS the joke |
| Fixed expression reinterpretation | Soul | Same face reads differently at different zoom levels |
| Vehicle | Skin | Monkey plush → any object/animal with fixed smug face |
| Setting | Skin | Bed/shelf → any domestic background |
| Background details | Skin | Wall color, furniture, decor |
| Exact pun wording | Skin | "Monkey behavior" → adapt to new vehicle + product |
| Number of zoom steps | Skin | 3 builds → 2-4 builds |

**Transferability**: Products that FIT: automation tools, AI products, anything where the vehicle can mock the "old way" of doing things. Products that BREAK: products with no clear "replaces manual effort" angle; products that can't form a pun with the vehicle.

**Lesson**: Not all slideshows need per-slide visual gags. Tension-Punchline uses images as pacing devices. Key requirements: (1) strong curiosity hook, (2) tension-building with visual continuity, (3) punchline funny enough to reward swiping.

## Case 007: Classical Painting Procrastination Meme (soft-ad for Paperpal)

**Archetype**: Tension-Punchline (compressed) | **Trigger**: Emotional (meme) + Social (identity) | **Info Format**: Comparison (accuser vs accused) | **Visual Continuity**: Strong (single-image split)

**Format**: 2 slides, portrait (~3:4), classical painting reproductions (Honore Daumier courtroom series)

| # | Role | Content | Text |
|---|------|---------|------|
| 0 | Hook | Daumier courtroom: dramatic lawyer gesturing accusingly with outstretched arms | "She said she came to study but she's been on TikTok for 45mins" |
| 1 | Spike / Soft-ad | Daumier courtroom: seated woman with resigned expression, lawyer's arm pointing from left edge. Paperpal logo + website screenshot overlaid. | "Paperpal helped me write and submit my paper 2x faster." + "I deserve a little break" |

**Why it works**: Classical art provides built-in gravitas that contrasts with any modern, trivial subject. The more dramatic the painting, the funnier the mundane text becomes.

**Structural blueprint** (what you can't remove):
- **Two different characters** with accuser→accused relationship, separated by the crop
- **Single-image split (strong continuity)**: The painting is one continuous scene cropped into two frames. The lawyer's arm crosses the slide boundary. Swiping = panning from accuser to accused.
- **Ad space**: The right side of the painting has a bright wall area above the seated figure — low information density region where the product screenshot sits naturally.
- **Ad-as-justification**: The product (Paperpal) is the reason the character "deserves a break" — the ad resolves the narrative tension.

```
Primary mechanism: "Say it for me" (Proxy Expression via Historical Anachronism)
- Viewer emotion: "I feel attacked but validated" — amused self-recognition
- Sharing motive: "This is literally me right now" — self-deprecation without shame
- Unspoken truth: "We all lie to ourselves about studying versus scrolling"

Secondary mechanism: "Dramatically unimportant" (Dignity Mismatch)
- Role: Elevating TikTok scrolling to 19th-century courtroom drama amplifies
  the humor and shareability
```

**Key tactics**:
- **Historical anachronism**: Classical fine art + modern slang = inherent dignity mismatch
- **Cross-slide gesture**: Lawyer's pointing arm crosses the crop boundary, making swipe feel like camera pan
- **Compressed 2-slide format**: Setup and payoff in just two beats
- **Third-person hook**: "She said..." creates observer perspective

### Soul vs Skin

| Element | Soul/Skin | Value |
|---------|:---------:|-------|
| Trigger type | Soul | Emotional (meme) + Social (identity) |
| Primary mechanism | Soul | Proxy Expression via Historical Anachronism |
| Secondary mechanism | Soul | Dignity Mismatch |
| Archetype | Soul | Tension-Punchline (compressed) |
| Information Format | Soul | Comparison (accuser → accused) |
| Visual Continuity | Soul | Strong (single-image split) — cross-slide gesture is essential |
| Linguistic pattern | Soul | Third-person accusation ("She said... but...") |
| Character relationship | Soul | Accuser → accused separated by crop |
| Ad insertion strategy | Skin | Ad-as-resolution (could also be Ad-as-reward) |
| Vehicle | Skin | Daumier courtroom → any dramatic classical painting with 2+ characters |
| Specific accusation | Skin | "Been on TikTok for 45mins" → any relatable procrastination |
| Product | Skin | Paperpal → any productivity tool |
| Text format | Skin | Caption Bar |

**Transferability**: Works for any product that can justify "I deserve a break" or "I already did the work". BREAKS for products unrelated to the accusation topic (the product must resolve the tension).

**Lesson**: Classical art is a powerful meme vehicle because it provides built-in gravitas. The "historical anachronism" tactic can be applied to any relatable modern behavior.

Only difference: Slide 4 product is "xxx" instead of "MuseOn", with copy "Using xxx to survive the human world" instead of "Using MuseOn to reverse-engineer viral formulas".

| # | Role | Content | Text |
|---|------|---------|------|

| 4 | Soft-ad | Hamster from behind, sitting in bed looking at iPad showing code/dev interface | "Using xxx to survive the human world" |

**Text Layer**: Overlay (white text on image, platform-level)

**Why it works**: Identical mechanism to Case 001. The hamster is a safe proxy for workplace rebellion. Three-layer contrast stacking (Scale + Species-role + Context) creates visual comedy base; the spike slide adds Behavior dimension (middle finger = Tier S social taboo). The grid preview hook lets viewers identify content type at thumbnail stage.

```
Trigger type(s): Emotional / Instinct (meme/humor)

Secondary mechanism: "That doesn't belong there" (Absurd Intrusion)

  Cute + rude = funnier than just rude.
```

### Commercial Analysis

- **Ad location**: Slide 4, full frame (hamster from behind looking at tablet)
- **Strategy**: Ad-as-final-slide — pure narrative slides followed by separate ad slide with different visual treatment (back view, private bedroom vs public settings)
- **Narrative justification**: "After enduring work, fitness, commute, using this product to survive the human world." Product = coping tool for daily grind.
- **Subtlety score**: 4 (overt — ad separated from narrative, copy directly mentions product, visual style shifts)
- **Product-mechanism fit**: Moderate. Product is not part of the punchline. Any tech/productivity/entertainment product could fill this slot.

**Key tactics**:

2. **Spike-first ordering**: Strongest slide (middle finger) at Slide 1, not last — ensures impact even if viewers only see two slides

To improve: place product within a narrative slide scene (Ad-as-background) instead of a separate slide.
